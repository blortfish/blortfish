Desktop Computer VS The Laptop

Being that this is a computer oriented class, it would seem only fair that we evaluate the pros and cons of both the larger desktop model of computers that we find in labs versus the now smaller and smaller laptops that we can carry with us to every class.

First off, I will discuss the pros for having a desktop computer. I personally own both a desktop and an array of laptops which I use all of. A desktop is a power station. You can get a number of different sizes that will fit whatever you could want. It allows you to keep data in a much more safe environment as well. The box will generally stay in one area and you will not be moving it while the platter is spinning. Desktop computers are also much more cost effective versus power. If you want 16GB or RAM, you can afford that quite easily on a desktop with the correct motherboard. That brings me to my next point, they are much easier to customize for the everyday user. You can open them up clean them, add, remove, and change really anything you want with relative ease. Your graphics can also be much more powerful. You have the space to hold a much larger graphics card if you want to run a crazy video game such as Battlefield 3 without spending a fortune, you need a graphics card to handle it. The availability of screen space is also a large plus for programmers and web designers. Having the screen space to open more than one application and be able to look back and forth makes your life much easier.

When it comes to cons on a desktop there are a few. The biggest one being the ability to move easily. You can't exactly carry your desktop, screen, and peripherals with you from class to class. It is not efficient and it is not easy. The amount of space it takes up could also be a con for someone constrained to a setting such as a dorm or a very small apartment. Power consumption is another concern with larger desktops and if you have multiple screens that are left on constantly.

Laptops can also serve the same purpose as a desktop, however they come with their own set of rules and constraints. Generally speaking, if you are looking for power it will become much more expensive and you will not be able to fit as much inside of a small laptop. Battery life can be an issue if you have too large of a screen. Contrary to a desktop, you may find it much harder to get inside of a laptop and fix any issues that you have with hardware. It of course depends on brand, but generally speaking it is much harder to get at the internals of a laptop. On the positive side of laptops is portability and power. Although you will not be able to have the same power as a desktop, it offers much more power than your smart phone or tablet. You have full access to applications that you had at home and you can take it all with you. No wires to connect to the internet leaves you completely untied from a wall. Another positive aspect of a laptop is the ability to watch your privacy. If you are going into a computer lab, you are not in full control of your data. Sure you can say that the admin is a trustworthy person, but you cannot be sure if everyone in that lab possesses the same intention. A hardware or software keylogger could be active without you even knowing it. The laptop ensures you know what is going on with your data.

Overall, both desktops and laptops have their places in our lives. The largest difference perhaps being power versus portability. Accompanied by interfacing and overall usability each presents their own advantages and disadvantages to achieve the same data output and processing.
